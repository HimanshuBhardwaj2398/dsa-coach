Heap / Priority Queue, explained

When you only ever need the smallest (or largest) item of a changing collection, a heap gives it in O(1) and takes an insert or removal in O(log n). Keep k items, and the root is the k-th best.

Spot it

A heap is a complete binary tree stored in a list: the children of index k sit at 2k + 1 and 2k + 2, and every parent is ≤ its children. So the root heap[0] is the minimum, and a push or pop repairs one root-to-leaf path: O(log n).

The pattern is repeatedly take the best of a changing set: the k largest of a stream, the next meeting to end, the next closest node, the smallest head of k sorted lists. Python's heapq is a min-heap; negate keys for a max-heap, and push (key, tiebreak, item) tuples when items don't compare.

Ask yourself

  1. Do I need the best item repeatedly while items change? If you need it only once, a scan or a sort is simpler.
  2. Do I need all n items, or only the best k? A heap of size k: O(n log k) time and O(k) space.
  3. Smallest or largest? heapq is a min-heap. To keep the k largest, keep a min-heap of size k: its root is the one to evict.
  4. Two halves at once? A median needs a max-heap of the lower half and a min-heap of the upper half.

Looks like it, isn't

  • The k most frequent elements. A heap of size k works; bucket sort by frequency is O(n). Either is fine in an interview.
  • The minimum of a sliding window. A heap needs lazy deletion; a monotonic deque does it in O(n).
  • Sorted output of everything. Just sort. A heap pays off when items arrive or change between the times you need the best.

What stays true

After each value, the heap holds exactly the k largest values seen so far (fewer at the start), and its root is the smallest of them.

Why each move is safe

  • A popped value is the smallest of k + 1 candidates: at least k seen values are larger, so it can never be among the k largest. Dropping it is safe.
  • Sift up only swaps a child with a larger parent, so after the swap the parent is the smaller of the two and the heap property holds above and below.
  • The tree is complete, so its height is ⌊log₂ n⌋: a sift touches at most that many levels.

Relies on: a total order on the keys. With tuples, Python compares the next field on a tie: add a counter before items that don't compare.

The family

Each member asks the heap for the best item while the set changes. What differs is what is kept and how keys are built.

A max-heap for the lower half (negated) and a min-heap for the upper half; keep their sizes within one. The median is a root, or the mean of both roots.

Where it breaks

  • Forgetting that heapq is a min-heap returns the smallest when you wanted the largest. Negate keys for a max-heap.
  • Pushing (priority, item) when items don't compare raises TypeError on a tie. Push (priority, counter, item).
  • Keeping all n items when k would do costs O(n) space and O(n log n) time instead of O(n log k).
  • Building a heap with n pushes is O(n log n); heapq.heapify(list) is O(n).
